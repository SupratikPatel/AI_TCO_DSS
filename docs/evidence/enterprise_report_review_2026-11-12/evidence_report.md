## Priority 3 — Julien Chaumond's Pi Hybrid Router vs. Published Pi Virtual Models

### 3.1 What Is Proven in Official Pi Documentation & Published Router Packages
* **Pi Virtual Models Architecture ([`pi.dev/docs/latest/virtual-models`](https://pi.dev/docs/latest/virtual-models)):** Introduced in Pi `v0.63.0` (current tested release `Pi 1.4.0`), `pi.registerVirtualModel()` registers a synthetic model ID whose `resolve(context)` callback runs **before every agent turn** (and on compaction boundaries) to select a concrete backend model based on the conversation state, prompt complexity, tool history, or external health checks.
* **`@caph42/pi-jev-router` (`v2.0.0`, Nov 1, 2026 — [`pi.dev/packages/@caph42/pi-jev-router`](https://pi.dev/packages/@caph42/pi-jev-router)):**
  * Registers virtual model **`jev/auto`** (`Auto (Jev)`) and routes across three physical slots: `models.complex` (default `openai-codex/gpt-6.1-sol`, `high` thinking), `models.standard` (default `openai-codex/gpt-6.1-terra`), and `models.implementation` (default `openai-codex/gpt-6-luna`, `max` thinking).
  * **Supported Classifier Backends (`classifier.provider`):**
    1. `typesafe-system-one` (`typesafe/jev-latest`, default; `~0.5 s` latency, `~$0.00006` per prompt for `~1.4K` tokens per [`@sugarforever/pi-jev-router`](https://pi.dev/packages/@sugarforever/pi-jev-router?page=3)).
    2. `llama-cpp-classify` (**local chat model running on a `llama.cpp` server**, using `/tokenize`, `/apply-template`, and `/completion` with `classifier.temperature` logit division to calibrate overconfident local label probabilities).
    3. `cloudflare-workers-ai-system-one` (`@cf/cloudflare/clef`) and `openai-decisions` (`openai/gpt-6-luna`).
  * **Exact Routing & Handoff Rules:**
    * **Upgrade to `models.complex`:** When classifier complex probability `p >= threshold` (`0.50` default).
    * **Downgrade to `models.standard`:** When `p <= adaptive.downgradeThreshold` (`0.15` default) after `adaptive.minDwellTurns` (`2` user turns).
    * **Guarded Handoff to `models.implementation`:** Triggered after a successful `edit` or `write` tool call when `p <= adaptive.handoffThreshold` (`0.20`), estimated context `<= adaptive.maxHandoffContextTokens` (`50,000` tokens), and input SHA-256 matches.
    * **Local Deadline & Fallback:** `adaptive.decisionTimeoutMs = 3000` ms (`maxRetries = 0`); if classification fails or times out on turn 1, falls back to `models.standard`, or keeps the current model on subsequent turns.
* **`pi-jev-task-router` ([`pi.dev/packages/pi-jev-task-router`](https://pi.dev/packages/pi-jev-task-router?name=%25s&page=95)):** Uses the `before_agent_start` hook to classify each prompt via `POST /v1/systemone` (4 questions in 1 call) and calls `pi.setModel()`, degrading to an offline keyword heuristic when `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` is absent or fails.

## Direct Answers to the Six Closing Questions

### 1. What is actually proven about Revolut's 56-B200 deployment?
In his October 13, 2026 AI Engineer NYC presentation ([`https://ai.engineer/nyc/2026/talks/revolut`](https://ai.engineer/nyc/2026/talks/revolut), `5:36:54–5:48:28`), Revolut AI Operations & Infrastructure Leader David Abitbol established that Revolut serves a tracked sample of **~2,000 internal software engineers** (distinct from its 80 million banking customers) on **56 NVIDIA B200 GPUs** hosted via **Together AI Dedicated Model Inference** (confirmed by the slide footnote inspected in [`xjTg_-6gpFA` @ `00:11–01:05`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xjTg_-6gpFA)). Revolut migrated from rationed `Claude Code` (`~$300/mo/engineer` cap) to unrationed open-weight **`GLM 5.1`** in April 2026 and upgraded to full text-only **`GLM 5.3`** (in **`NVFP4` / `FP4`**, capped at **`256K` context**, targeting **`>90%` prefix cache hit rate**) by August–October 2026, while UI designers use multimodal **`GLM 5.3 Flash`** and a local router inside `OpenCode`/`Codex` routes image inputs to **`GPT-6 Luna`** and keeps frontier APIs as exceptions. On that fixed 56-B200 fleet, tuning speculative decoding, sequential prefill, and prefill/decode disaggregation between April and August 2026 increased sustained traffic from **`~3 RPS` to `>7 RPS` (`+76%`)** while cutting TTFT by **`-88%` from `14.26 s` to `1.68 s`**. However, the exact number of model replicas, server count, and prefill/decode GPU split remain **`UNKNOWN`** (Abitbol notes at `5:44:31` that `TP8` is preferred over `TP4` for KV-cache capacity, but never states `7 replicas`).

### 2. Which numbers from Revolut are measured facts, and which are external estimates?
* **Measured / Primary Reported Facts from Revolut ([`ai.engineer/nyc/2026/talks/revolut`](https://ai.engineer/nyc/2026/talks/revolut)):**
  * **Fleet size:** `56` NVIDIA B200 GPUs (`5:41:18`, `5:43:23`).
  * **Tracked engineering sample:** `~2,000` software engineers (`5:38:12`, `5:41:18`).
  * **April → August 2026 tuning on 56 B200s:** Request rate rose from **`~3 RPS` to `>7 RPS` (`+76%`)**; TTFT dropped from **`14.26 s` to `1.68 s` (`-88%`)** (`5:42:54–5:43:43`).
  * **Latency thresholds:** Internal target **`<3 s` TTFT**; user complaint threshold **`>5 s` TTFT** (`5:41:31–5:41:44`).
  * **Exact meaning of `200 tok/s` vs `800 tok/s`:** **`~200 tok/s`** is per-user interactive streaming speed during business hours; **`800 tok/s`** is single-user decode speed **after 5:00 PM when one engineer is alone on a GPU** (`5:41:46–5:42:12`).
  * **Token economics:** **`$0.30` per 1M blended tokens** (counting `>90%` cached prefix hits at `$0`), representing **`~1/10th` (`10%`)** of prior Anthropic/OpenAI spend (`5:42:17–5:42:25`).
* **External Unverified Estimates (Not Revolut Facts):**
  * **`$164k–$198k/month` ([Andevo LinkedIn Post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/andevo_revolut-rents-56-nvidia-b200-gpus-164k-share-7524084235712925696-YJ6v/)):** External calculation assuming `~$4.00–$4.85/GPU/hr` bare B200 rental.
  * **`~$367,511/month` (`$183.76/engineer/mo`) or `~$278,000/month` (`$139/engineer/mo`) ([`xjTg_-6gpFA` @ `01:25–02:30`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xjTg_-6gpFA)):** External multiplication of `56 B200s × 730 hrs` by Together AI's public `$8.99/hr/B200` Dedicated Inference list price or `~$6.80/hr` bare cluster pricing.

### 3. What is the best public multi-request shared-GPU benchmark for coding/agentic workloads?
Three complementary primary benchmarks provide the strongest public evidence across datacenter and workstation GPUs:
1. **`Qwen3.8-Flash-Next` on `8× NVIDIA H100` using the SemiAnalysis `AgentX` Multi-Turn Coding Harness ([Backboard / InferenceX, Nov 1, 2026](https://backboard.io/blog/qwen-flash-next-h100-full-precision)):** Tests real multi-turn coding-agent sessions with **`87,000–106,000` average input tokens per request** across **`c = 1, 4, 8, 12, 16`** concurrent sessions, reporting total throughput (`9,914` vs `8,013 tok/s/GPU` at `c=16`), output throughput (`87.1` vs `72.2 tok/s/GPU` at `c=16`), p50/p90 TTFT (`1.21 s` vs `3.50 s` p90 at `c=16`), and p90 per-user streaming speed (`54.0` vs `4.2 tok/s` at `c=12`; `47.6` vs `14.4 tok/s` at `c=16`).

### 4. What is actually proven about Julien Chaumond's Pi router?

### 6. What is the strongest verified low-bit Qwen 3.8 Flash Next quality result for tool calling or coding?
* **Strongest Sub-3-Bit Tool-Calling Result:** On the **100-scenario multi-step `Tool-eval-bench 3.0`** suite ([Sushi Engine GitHub](https://github.com/beamivalice/sushi)), **`Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-Sushi-STQ-2.75bpw`** (`44.9 GiB` weights) scored **`94 / 100` (`94.0%`)**, outperforming its 4-bit `Sushi-4bpw` counterpart (`90 / 100`).
* **Strongest 3-Bit Coding Result (`LiveCodeBench v6`):** In [ISTA-DASLab's `Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RCO-GGUF` evaluations](https://huggingface.co/ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RCO-GGUF) (which keep the 51.2B `per_layer_token_embd` n-gram shard at `4.50 bpw IQ4_NL` = `28.8 GB`), **`IQ3_S` (`3.50 bpw` transformer, `83.6 GB` total)** scored **`86.86%` on `LiveCodeBench v6`** (`-0.57 pp` vs `BF16`'s `87.43%`) and **`93.26%` 3-task average** (`100.0%` `AIME25`, `92.93%` `GPQA-Diamond`), while **`IQ3_XXS` (`3.00 bpw` transformer, `75.8 GB` total)** scored **`86.29%` on `LiveCodeBench v6`** (`-1.14 pp` vs `BF16`).
* **Strongest 2-Bit / Sub-2-Bit Agentic Coding Result & Floor Warning:** In [Unsloth's Dynamic v3.1 evaluations](https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/dynamic-3.0-ggufs/dynamic-3.1-ggufs) (`113` `DeepSWE v1.1` tasks × 3 passes and `82` `Terminal-Bench 2.1` tasks × 3 passes), even **`UD-IQ1_S` (`66.4 GB`)** reached **`54.0%` on `DeepSWE v1.1`** (vs `58.7%` `BF16`) and **`68.3%` on `Terminal-Bench 2.1`**, while `ISTA-DASLab`'s **`IQ2_XS` (`68.0 GB`)** and **`Q2_0` (`66.4 GB`)** scored **`83.43%`** and **`81.14%`** on `LiveCodeBench v6` (and **`714/1,000 = 71.4%`** on `MMLU-Pro` in [`oMLX mkzszvig`](https://omlx.ai/benchmarks/intelligence/mkzszvig)).
